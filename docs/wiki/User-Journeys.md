# User journeys

## 1. Research and executive kickoff

An advisor researches a company's offering, industry, customers, and public structure. Industry mapping belongs here when there is enough information. Public information is provisional.

The point-of-contact invitation asks leadership to bring the people who should weigh in, a department/people roster, reporting lines, responsibilities, goals, and perceived problems. A CSV roster can arrive before or after kickoff. Validate identifiers, emails, managers, and reporting relationships before treating the chart as accurate.

The kickoff guide uses available research and replies. Do not require selecting nonexistent supplier walkthroughs or previously completed interviews to prepare the first meeting.

## 2. Team discovery

Leadership context establishes people, roles, departments, and proposed duties. Advisors review those dossiers and create tailored questions.

Questions should elicit multiple repeatable tasks, not merely a broad duty. For each task, ask about the trigger, incoming data and source, actions, software, exceptions, output, recipient, and upstream/downstream dependencies. Six to ten tasks might be plausible for an example; it is not an extraction quota.

An explicit send action delivers a branded invitation with a private link. Voice is preferred because examples reveal steps and frustrations; typing remains available.

## 3. Participant review

The participant records or types an account, checks the transcript, and asks AI to create task cards. Cards should be readable SOP-like descriptions: person/role, duty, input, ordered actions, output, handoff, and software. Missing details remain visible instead of being invented.

The primary card actions are Approve and Edit. Approval needs visible feedback. Editing must preserve the meaning of the reviewed version and require review of changed content. The final submission returns the original account and reviewed descriptions.

**Approval means “this is my best understanding of my work.”** It does not establish exclusive ownership, authorize automation, or resolve another person's conflicting account.

## 4. Advisor review and client readout

The advisor sees returned participants and their task cards, with transcripts collapsed. The useful questions are: who owns this, is a handoff missing, do accounts conflict, and could a bounded part benefit from AI?

The company work map provides a stable place to discuss flows, responsibilities, task details, and recorded handoffs. Findings must be presented as hypotheses where evidence or measurement is incomplete.

The intended next meeting presents the map, findings, ownership decisions, proposed improvements, and a follow-up review. The system must not claim it has proven the bottleneck merely because it found a missing owner.

## 5. Candidate delegation

An advisor identifies a task candidate. Governance then evaluates the person, permissions, task boundaries, policy, conflicts, and reviewer authority before any issuance. Discovery approval alone never grants scopes.

## Current implementation boundary

The real participant routes, provider-backed drafts, and public fictional walkthrough are separate implementations. The demo demonstrates a prepared story; it does not analyze arbitrary sample text or send real messages.

Cross-team analysis now has structural checks and an explicit AI review with citations and advisor decisions; see [team analysis](../team-analysis.md). Continuous analysis, automatic conflict resolution, and end-to-end governed execution remain future work. See [current state](Current-State.md) and [discovery reference](../pilot-discovery.md).
