# Product vision

## The problem

An org chart shows reporting lines. An identity system shows who someone is. An access system records entitlements. Advisors still need to understand what people actually do: which inputs they receive, what they produce, who depends on them, which systems they use, and where work breaks.

DutyGraph connects that operational understanding to strategy and possible AI delegation. The initial customer experience is an advisor helping a company understand and improve its work. The product should earn trust through a useful discovery outcome before asking the company to adopt more sophisticated governance.

## The product progression

| Phase | Intended value | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advisor-led discovery | Research, kickoff, team accounts, reviewed tasks, visible business map | Primary pilot product |
| Guided improvement | Find ownership gaps, improve handoffs, test priorities and AI candidates | Working review surfaces; customer validation needed |
| Embedded operating support | Advisor/strategist maintains the record over 6–12 months | Commercial hypothesis; not a contracted duration |
| Internal handoff | Train an internal owner to maintain the system | Training foundations exist; handoff program needs validation |
| Subscription and enterprise/channel licensing | Sustained value across companies and advisor ecosystems | Go-to-market direction, not proven distribution |
| Adaptive business | Continuous inputs inform strategy and proposed human/agent work | Long-term vision; no autonomous operating loop today |

Enterprise/channel distribution, including an ecosystem such as Telarus, is a target opportunity. It is not an established partnership, integration, endorsement, or signed licensing agreement.

## What makes the model useful

The unit of discovery is meaningful work. A person occupies a role, has duties, and performs granular tasks. Each task has an input, actions, an output, a destination, and systems. Duties can survive a personnel change; task descriptions should help a replacement understand the work.

The evidence must remain connected to the description. A polished diagram without explainable records is insufficient. A task inventory without actionable relationships is also insufficient.

AI should do the heavy lifting of extracting and organizing accounts. People should review and correct a readable draft. They should not have to retype their interview into another long form.

## Product principles

- Advisor first: default screens help discover, understand, and present.
- Voice preferred, typing supported: natural accounts often include exceptions and frustrations.
- Ask once, reuse evidence across frameworks.
- Show task cards first; retain transcripts behind an explicit disclosure.
- Make uncertainty visible without burying the user in technical language.
- Distinguish a person's understanding from agreed ownership, policy, and delegated authority.
- Keep the primary company map stable and readable. Use focused relationships for exploration.
- Ground recommendations in evidence and tests, not the visual size of a graph node.
- Keep customer data and simulated examples unmistakably separate.

## Commercial proof we need

The recruitment aim is 5–10 pilot companies willing to involve leadership and their teams. This is a target, not a verified cohort count. Measure completion, advisor effort, correction burden, useful findings, and time to an agreed action before promising speed or ROI.

A credible investment story requires repeatable customer value, trained advisors, defensible data handling, and clear operating costs. A larger feature list alone is not that proof.
